Emerging Conditions — clusters before they have a code

Emerging-condition surveillance (/emerging-conditions) is for the disease that has no ICD/SNOMED code yet — early COVID, early AIDS. A ProtoCondition is a living, versioned cluster of patients, signals, and hypotheses: you treat and track it locally, close the net as evidence accrues, and promote it to a real code when one arrives. The page is gated by the EMERGING_CONDITIONS feature; creating, editing, promoting, sweeping, confirming and excluding require the EPI MANAGER security key. Reads are open — surveillance is part of the chart, not a privacy silo.

The lifecycle

  1. Define a cluster: a name, then a case definition — features across symptoms, labs, vitals, diagnoses, demographics and exposures, each with a weight and a rule (weighted / must-have / disqualifier). Set a match threshold. Activate to go live.
  2. Screen: run a sweep, or let clinicians surface patients from the symptom survey. Matches arrive as candidates with a full per-feature evidence snapshot (✓ satisfied, ✗ assessed-but-absent, — not assessed).
  3. Review: confirm the candidates that belong and exclude those that don't. Confirming a member can trip a count-threshold alert to the infection-control team.
  4. Close the net: the Analytics tab shows each feature's cluster rate vs its background — a Signal discriminates the cluster, Noise occurs at background. Apply a suggested drop/raise, then re-sweep.
  5. Promote: when a code arrives, promote the cluster. The definition freezes, candidates expire, an eCR trigger is emitted, and every confirmed member is queued for problem-list recoding (with a citation back to the cluster).

Drafts proposed by clinicians

A treating clinician who screens a patient and finds that nothing matches can propose a draft cluster from that patient's record — the symptoms marked present, any etiologic tests that came back negative, and any abnormal results. Those arrive in your cluster list badged Draft, with the proposing patient already suggested as the index case.

A draft is inert: no chart shows it, no precaution banner fires, and no sweep runs against it until you activate it. Treat the features as a starting point rather than a case definition — a draft derived from a single patient fits that patient exactly and generalises to nothing, and it says so in its own description. Broaden the features and weights, set a sensible threshold, then activate.

Promotion is a one-way door

Promoting asks you to confirm, because it cannot be undone. The definition freezes permanently, every remaining candidate is expired, and there is no un-promote — a promoted cluster cannot even be retired. Set Effective from if you know when the condition began; it becomes the onset date on every recoded problem.

Each confirmed member then appears on a worklist with Recode and Skip. Nothing is written to a patient's chart until you click Recode — and a member you skip by mistake can be put back with Reopen.

What a Recode does to the chart. It adds the coded diagnosis with a structured citation back to the cluster, and — when the patient was carrying exactly one working diagnosis recorded as suspected, provisional or differential — marks that one superseded and links the two, so the chart reads as one condition with a history rather than two overlapping diagnoses. If the patient had no such working diagnosis, or more than one, the system does not guess: the coded problem is added on its own and the worklist records why. Retiring the wrong active diagnosis would be worse than leaving you two rows to reconcile.
A recode is not counted as a misdiagnosis. The code set moved; nobody was wrong. It stays out of the revision-rate numerator and out of its denominator, so promoting a cluster with hundreds of members cannot shift any figure the diagnostic stewardship advisory reports.
Between promotion and recode, a member's precaution banner stays up. They have no coded diagnosis yet, so removing the banner at that moment would leave an infection-control patient with neither. It stands down once their problem list carries the code.

Reading the analytics honestly

Rates divide by the patients you actually asked (the assessed denominator), never by the whole cohort — low survey compliance shows up as "not assessed", not as a false Absent. Lift is a documented heuristic with minimum-count guards, not a p-value, and the background source is labeled (assessed population, or the curated catalog when the population is too thin).
