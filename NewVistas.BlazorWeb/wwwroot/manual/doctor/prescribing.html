Prescribing & the Safety Gate

Writing outpatient prescriptions and choosing the patient's pharmacy, Electronic Prescribing of Controlled Substances (EPCS), and the patient-safety confirmation that stops a dangerous order before it's written.

Write an outpatient prescription

  1. Open Medications (or /medications) for the patient.
  2. Click + New Prescription.
  3. Enter the drug, sig, dosage, quantity, days supply and refills. The prescriber defaults to you.
  4. Confirm the Pharmacy: it defaults to the patient's preferred pharmacy — ask which pharmacy they'd like and click Change to search for a different one.
  5. Click Place Prescription. It's added to the active-med list and routed to the chosen pharmacy.
Choosing the pharmacy Modern Letting the patient pick any outpatient pharmacy is a Modern enhancement (flag EXTERNAL_PHARMACY, on by default) — beyond core VistA/RPMS, which dispense in-house plus CMOP mail-order. When it's on, patients can use any retail (CVS, Walgreens, …), mail-order, or specialty pharmacy; each has a default pharmacy they set in the Patient Portal under My Pharmacy, and new prescriptions default to it (override per prescription). With it off, prescriptions dispense from the facility pharmacy. Inpatient orders always go to the hospital pharmacy.
How it's sent Modern Outpatient e-prescriptions are transmitted to the chosen pharmacy using the national NCPDP SCRIPT standard over the Surescripts network — the same protocol for every chain (CVS, Walgreens, independents, mail-order). The demo build has no live Surescripts connection, so the prescription is created and the system records that it would route to the chosen pharmacy. Multiple-pharmacy support is a site option — the EXTERNAL_PHARMACY feature, on by default — that a VA/IHS-style site can switch off to dispense in-house.

Write an e-Prescription

  1. In the sidebar, open EPCS (or go to /epcs).
  2. Enter the patient ID and click Load.
  3. Click New e-Rx to open the prescription form.
  4. Fill in the drug, DEA schedule, quantity, days supply, sig, and prescriber details.
  5. Click Create e-Prescription. For a normal order it's written immediately and appears in the list.

The controlled-substance safety gate

When an order's daily dose is dangerously high for its DEA schedule, the system will not let it through on a single click. Instead it raises a Tier-2 confirmation that is deliberately designed to defeat muscle-memory: the safe choice (Cancel) is the primary, right-most button, and the override is secondary, on the left, and disabled until you prove you read the warning.

High dose — type-to-confirm

For a high-but-survivable dose, you may override — but only after typing the drug name exactly and recording an audited reason.

Lethal dose — co-signature required

For an order in the lethal range (for example, 100 tablets per day of a Schedule II opioid), a single prescriber cannot override it at all. The Override button is disabled and the order requires a pharmacist or attending co-signature.

⚠ Why this exists A prescription for 100 Nucynta per day should never be approvable by a reflexive click. The gate forces you to stop and read. Patient-safety friction here is intentional — it is enforced by the system, not left to a differently-colored button you might learn to ignore.

Daily dose = quantity ÷ days supply. The same gate guards dispensing at Pharmacy Point of Sale.

Normal prescribing is unaffected Routine orders (e.g. Oxycodone 5 mg every 6 hours = 4/day) never see the gate — it only appears for genuinely dangerous daily doses.
