Symptom Survey — the structured wide net

When a patient's illness may not have a name yet, the Symptom Survey (/symptom-survey) is how you cast the wide net: a coded review-of-systems where every answer is Present, Absent, or Unknown. Recording "asked and absent" is what lets an outbreak's case definition tighten later — you can only drop a symptom that turns out to be common if the survey actually asked about it.

Why three states, not free text

A symptom you dismiss in a free-text note is invisible, not absent — and invisible can't be analyzed. The survey makes the distinction explicit: Unknown means "not asked" and is never treated as absent. Leave an item Unknown only when it genuinely wasn't assessed.

Using it

  1. Enter the patient and Load. The question set is the core screen plus any symptoms the currently active clusters are watching, grouped by body system.
  2. Answer Present / Absent for what you assessed; leave the rest Unknown.
  3. Save answers. Only non-Unknown answers are recorded.
  4. Screen against active clusters to see whether this patient matches an emerging cluster, with a score. If they do, Suggest into cluster flags them for the epidemiologist to review — a human suggestion persists even if a later evaluation stops matching.
Recording symptoms needs a front-door key (PROVIDER, ORES, ORELSE, or EPI MANAGER) — nurses can do it. Once an epidemiologist confirms the patient into a cluster, an isolation precaution banner appears on the patient's cover sheet.
