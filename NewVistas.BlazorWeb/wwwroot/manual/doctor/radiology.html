Radiology & AI Findings

The Radiology screen (VistA File #75.1) shows a patient's studies, their filed reports, contrast and dose records, and critical-result handling. Once a report is filed, the study also offers AI finding extraction — a structured reading of what the radiologist wrote, with an acknowledgment gate on the findings that matter.

Why this exists

The dangerous radiology miss is rarely the headline finding — it is the second finding, two sentences later, in a busy report. A mild central stenosis at C5-C6 can mask a moderate-to-severe foraminal stenosis at the same level in the very next sentence. The extraction turns each such sentence into a discrete row a surgeon cannot skim past.

Extract findings

  1. Open a study whose report has been filed and click Extract findings from report in the AI Findings section. Nothing runs until you ask.
  2. Each extracted finding shows a severity chip, its type and level, laterality, and — always — the verbatim report sentence it came from.
Grounded, or flagged Every finding's quoted sentence is verified against the report text. A finding whose quote is not actually in the report is marked unverified quote rather than shown as an equal — the extraction can only surface what the radiologist wrote, never invent a finding.

The acknowledgment gate

Findings of moderate severity or worse are flagged as material and ask for a decision: Acknowledge (it is noted) or Reject… with a required reason. Milder findings carry no gate — scoping the forcing function to material findings is what keeps it from becoming alert fatigue.

A rejection is recorded — and patient-visible Rejecting a material finding requires a reason, and both the rejection and its reason are recorded in the chart and visible to the patient. Dismissing a finding is allowed; dismissing one carelessly is neither free nor invisible.

What this is not

  • Not an interpretation of the images. It reads the radiologist's own report text — nothing else.
  • Not a substitute for reading the report. The full report stays above the findings, and the disclaimer under the panel says so.
  • Not connected to the critical-results workflow. Critical-result notification (who was called, who acknowledged) remains the separate, human-driven section above.

Also on this screen

  • Contrast — agent, route, volume, and reaction recording.
  • Radiation dose — effective dose, CTDI, DLP.
  • Critical results — flag, notify, acknowledge.
  • Report actions — sign and amend.
